Names, before the mark.

Three ways to name a one-person studio that fires nine assets a week and files them with their source. Each candidate is short enough to say out loud, spell once, and set at 16px next to a search field.

I can't check registrars — treat the .com / .ai notes as a read on how likely a name is to be free, not a verdict. Shortlist three, run them, and I'll build the mark for whatever survives.

1A KEEP THE FIRE — CERAMIC TERMS

Same world as Kiln, deeper in the vocabulary. Obscure enough to still be free, and every one carries the idea of heat turning something soft into something permanent.

Sinter Heat that fuses loose powder into one solid body. Loose prompts into a working system. SINTER.AI
Bisque The first firing — finished, unglazed, ready for whatever you do to it next. BISQUE.AI
Anneal Slow cooling that takes the stress out of glass so it stops cracking. Work that holds up. ANNEAL.AI

1B THE VAULT — WHAT YOU'RE BUYING

Names about the archive itself: filed, provenanced, source included. Reads more product than studio — the safer choice if you ever sell seats to teams.

Shelfmark A library's code for exactly where a thing lives. Two hundred and forty of them. SHELFMARK.COM
1C THE RHYTHM — NINE, EVERY THURSDAY

Names built on the cadence rather than the craft. Highest availability of the three, because they're coined — and they make the weekly drop the whole story.

Ninefold Nine assets a week, folded in. States the promise in the name. NINEFOLD.AI
Thursdayworks The drop day as the brand. Long, but nobody forgets when to check back. THURSDAYWORKS.COM
Firedrop Keeps the heat and adds the cadence. The one that works as a verb. FIREDROP.AI

MY THREE

Sinter, Shelfmark, Ninefold. Sinter keeps everything the current site already says about heat and permanence in one two-syllable word. Shelfmark is the one that scales if this ever becomes a product for teams. Ninefold is the safest bet on availability.
